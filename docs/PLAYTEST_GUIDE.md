# Playtest Guide — the Devil's Draw slice

**For:** the owner's first full playthrough of Milestones 1–4.
**Run:** `cd web && npm run godot:export && npm run dev`, then open
http://localhost:5174. Click once anywhere to enable sound.

A season is 14 days. It takes roughly 30–60 minutes, depending on how long you
stay at the table. To start over at any time: ⚙ → Start a new game.

## What happens

1. **The arrival.** You owe The Dealer 250 gold by day 14, and you have 1,000
   silver.
2. **The Rusty Spur.** Play the drifter heads-up. Watch his portrait: a bad
   enough beating (suck-outs, caught bluffs, being pushed off hands) leaves
   him dusty and disheveled, and while he looks like that he plays looser.
3. **His deed.** Once he's down to half his chips, he may stake his silver
   claim against 500 of your silver. Win it and it pays 150 silver every
   morning.
4. **Town business.** Walk the street with WASD or the arrow keys and press E
   at a door, or use the Places menu. The assay office trades silver for gold
   (the rate follows the town's mood) and sends payments to The Dealer. The
   Rusty Spur has the table and the House Line. The boarding house is where
   you sleep to end the day. Each morning autosaves.
5. **Crane.** Reach 3,000 silver in net worth and Augustus Crane invites you
   to his riverboat. Or let the marker go past day 14 and he summons you.
   Pick standard or double stakes, then play a heads-up freezeout.
6. **After.** Play continues whether you win or lose. The Journal button shows the
   story so far.

## What to tell me afterward

The milestone's exit test is whether you can **explain how a poker decision
changed a character and the town, and keep playing after a win or a loss.**
Beyond that, anything that bugged you counts. These are especially useful:

- **The drifter's look.** Did you notice it change? Did it feel fair and worth
  acting on, or too sticky (unkempt for too long) or too subtle?
- **Money.** Was 1,000 silver and 250 gold by day 14 tense, impossible, or
  easy? Did the assay office and House Line make sense without explanation?
- **The deed.** Did the drifter ever offer it? Was 500 silver a fair price?
- **Crane.** Did he feel like a boss, different from the drifter? Were the
  stakes clear before you sat down?
- **Feel.** Readability, sound, pacing (hands, waits between actions), and
  anything confusing on any screen.

Numbers are all data and quick to change: thresholds, prices, stakes, how
fast the drifter tilts and recovers.
